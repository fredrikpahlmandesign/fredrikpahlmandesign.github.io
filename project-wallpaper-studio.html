01 — Origin

A wallpaper on a refurbished phone

I bought a refurbished iPhone recently and it came with a wallpaper from a few years back — wavy, grainy, satisfying. Something about the texture stayed with me.

I wanted to recreate it. But instead of generating it with AI and reprompting a hundred times, I took a different approach: I asked Claude to build me a tool with sliders.

The insight: if you can describe the controls of a visual system, you can explore a much wider design space than iterating prompts — and get exactly where you want to go.

This is increasingly how I think about AI in design workflows. Don't ask for an output. Ask for a machine that produces outputs.

02 — The tool

Sliders instead of prompts

I described the visual system — ridges, lighting, grain, curve shapes — and Claude built the UI. The controls became the creative interface.

The tool generates ridge-based wallpapers through a Canvas renderer with full parametric control: ridge count, amplitude, center position, tilt, curve shape (cubic, wave, arch), organic warp, edge sharpness, shadow depth, light direction, and light elevation.

Four colour presets — Titanium, Obsidian, Sahara, Slate — plus custom colour controls. A Shuffle button generates random combinations. Export at full resolution.

03 — Exploration

Unexpected patterns emerged

Once the tool was running, something interesting happened. Adjusting ridge count didn't just scale the original — it generated genuinely different aesthetics.

One ridge: a single soft wave, almost architectural. Four ridges: flowing dunes. Five: something that started to look braided. Each combination of curve shape, center offset, and lighting felt like a new material.

04 — Presets

Titanium, Obsidian, Sahara, Slate

As favourite combinations settled, they became presets — named after the material they resembled most.

The Shuffle button randomises all parameters at once, for when you want to break out of what you know looks good. The export crops to full phone resolution.

The light controls (direction, elevation, shadow depth) push the ridges from graphic to tactile — small changes in angle change whether the surface reads as matte, specular, or deeply textured.

05 — In use

On the lock screen

The whole point was always to end up with something I'd actually put on my phone. The wallpaper I started with is still there — but now next to a few I made myself.

06 — Reflection

The method

Building a parametric tool instead of prompting directly is increasingly how I approach AI-assisted design. It shifts the creative work to where it belongs — in the designer's hands — and removes the bottleneck of prompt iteration.

The tool becomes a creative instrument. You play it, rather than negotiate with it.

What I'd do differently

The colour system is still fairly manual — selecting from four presets plus custom. A future version could derive the colour palette from a reference image, giving you the texture of the tool with the palette of something you already love.

This is the same approach I used for Bubble Studio — the two projects are siblings.
